Method

How every number is worked out

GradeA to E
Each deal is scored on ten tests against your buy box. A test met counts 1, a test within reach counts ½. A = 90%+, B = 75%+, C = 55%+, D = 35%+, E below that. A and B fit your buy box.
1% ruleQuick screen
Monthly rent ÷ price. A 10-second first screen. At South African interest rates few apartments reach 1%, so anything near it is strong.
50% ruleQuick screen
Running costs usually take about half the rent. The expense ratio shows your own estimate. Far below 50%? Check for missing costs.
Cash flow, cap rate, DSCRQuick screen
NOI is rent collected less running costs. Cap rate is NOI ÷ price. Cash flow is NOI − bond payments. DSCR is NOI ÷ bond payments; lenders like 1.2 or more.
Gross and effective yieldYield & return
Gross yield is annual rent ÷ price. Effective yield is NOI ÷ total cost, including transfer duty, fees and upfront repairs.
Cash-on-cash (ROI)Yield & return
Annual cash flow ÷ cash you put in: deposit, transfer duty, attorney and bond fees, repairs. The default target is 12%.
Buying below valueYield & return
(market value − price) ÷ market value. Much of a property's return can be made on the way in: buying from a motivated seller, or fixing up an under-rented property.
Annualised returnYield & return
IRR over your hold: rent growth, cost growth, bond paydown, capital growth and selling costs.
Stress test
Each scenario reruns the full model with one input made worse. Breaking points solve for the exact rate, rent or vacancy where cash flow hits zero. Offer prices solve for the highest price that still meets your target, with duty and fees recalculated; levies and rates are held fixed.
Income vs expenses
Twenty years of rent collected (after vacancy) against running costs and bond repayments, using your rent and levy growth rates. The bond disappears once it's paid off. "Cash you top up" adds up every year the rent falls short. The sale of the property is left out.
Present value
Each year's net cash from the 20-year view, discounted back to today: Σ net ÷ (1 + rate)^year, with cash treated as arriving at each year end. The default rate is prime (set under Market), the return you'd get by paying down debt instead. Use your own required return if it's higher.
Tax
Add your marginal rate on a deal to see after-tax cash flow. Taxable rental income is NOI less bond interest; a loss usually reduces tax on other income. Grades use pre-tax numbers.
Before you offerChecklist
  • TPN report on tenant payment behaviour in the area
  • Levy statement and any planned special levies
  • Municipal account arrears
  • Title deed and approved building plans
  • Current tenant's status and arrears
Model checks
The engine is tested against an independent month-by-month bond simulation, the SARS transfer duty table, hand-built cash flows, and thousands of random and junk deals for consistency.

Transfer duty uses the SARS table effective 1 April 2025, unchanged for 2026/27 (last checked 28 September 2026). On new developments the developer usually pays duty and fees. Attorney and bond fees are estimates; get a quote. A calculator, not financial advice.

What the grade means

A quick screen, not a verdict on the property

Every deal gets a grade from A to E. It shows how well the deal meets your buy box: ten targets for cash flow, yield and return. Change a target and every grade updates.

Each test that is met scores 1 point. A test that is close scores half a point: within 20% of the target (15% for the expense ratio, where lower is better), or within R1,000 a month for cash flow. A test that can't be worked out is left out. For example, "below market value" needs a market value. The grade is the share of points scored.

A90% or more. Fits your buy box
B75% or more. Fits your buy box
C55% or more
D35% or more
EBelow 35%

The ten tests

With your current targets

The quick screens are fast first checks that many rental investors use. The yield and return tests look at what the deal earns on the money you put in. The default targets are common rules of thumb, not recommendations. Set your own in Buy box & method.

What the grade leaves out

The grade uses first-year, pre-tax numbers and only what you enter. It can't see the building's condition, the area, the tenant, the body corporate's finances or your own circumstances. A good grade means a deal is worth a closer look, not that you should buy it. The stress test and 20-year view show how a deal holds up when things go wrong. The checklist under Before you offer lists what to check next.
